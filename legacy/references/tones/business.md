# Business Briefing Tone

Use this tone for executive briefings, internal presentations, project updates, and corporate decision-forcing decks. NOT for academic lectures (use `academic.md`), NOT for keynotes / pitches (use `keynote.md`).

## Voice and register

- **Answer-first / Pyramid (Minto).** Lead every section with the conclusion; subsequent slides are evidence supporting it. Executives skim; the headline must carry the claim.
- **Active, declarative, present tense.** "We recommend X" not "It is recommended that X". "Margins improved 4%" not "There has been improvement in margins of approximately 4%".
- **Concrete numbers everywhere.** Every claim needs a number, a name, or a date. "Sales declined" is weak; "Q3 sales declined 12% vs Q2" is the standard. If you don't have a number, find one or drop the claim.
- **First-person plural with restraint.** "We recommend" is fine; "we feel", "we believe" weak; replace with "data shows", "X indicates".

## What the deck should NEVER do

- **Bury the lede.** If the recommendation is on slide 12, the deck is broken. Move it to slide 1.
- **Hedge unnecessarily.** Executives interpret hedging as evasion. Reserve "may" and "appears to" for genuinely uncertain claims.
- **Use academic citation conventions.** Inline `(Author Year)` clutters the slide. Use footnotes or speaker-notes citations only.
- **Run long.** Briefing decks beyond 30 slides for a 30-minute slot fail; aim for ≤ 0.7 slides/min.

## Structure

The dominant skeleton is the **answer-first pyramid**:

1. **Executive summary** (1–2 slides) — the recommendation, the headline number, the timeframe.
2. **Three supporting arguments** (3–5 slides each) — each argued in pyramid form: claim → evidence → implications.
3. **Risks / counter-arguments** (1–2 slides) — credibility-building; acknowledge the case against.
4. **Decision ask** (1 slide) — what specifically you need from the audience, with a date.

## Citations

- **Footnoted on slide** with a small numeric superscript ¹²³ keyed to a sources list on the last slide.
- **No academic-style inline `(Author Year)`** — too cluttered for the format.
- Internal data uses an internal-source citation: *"Source: Q3 financial close, accounts.local"*.

## Pacing

- **0.5–1.0 slides/min**. A 30-minute briefing fits 15–30 slides; a 60-minute board meeting 30–60.
- **Decisions slot** at the end gets ≥ 5 minutes alone — don't rush the ask.

## Worked examples

Customer cases, not academic problems. *"Acme's 2024 rollout: 6-month timeline, $4M budget, 18% adoption"*. Concrete, named, dated.

## Active engagement

- **Predict the number** before revealing it ("Anyone want to guess what Q3 came in at?") — works for budget reviews.
- **Defer questions** to a "questions" slot rather than mid-flow, unless the briefing is small (< 8 people).
