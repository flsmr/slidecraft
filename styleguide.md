# Visual Content Style Guide for IUG / ILSE-Style Slides

## 1. Overall visual character
Create slide content in a clean, editorial, Swiss-inspired corporate style. The design should feel structured, minimal, modern, and slightly bold, with strong contrast between black, white, and one vivid accent color. Avoid decorative effects, gradients, shadows, rounded cards, glossy surfaces, or overly playful illustration styles. The content should look like it belongs in a professional university or institutional presentation: precise, sober, confident, and highly legible. Use a restrained layout language: large areas of white space; strong typographic hierarchy; rectangular blocks and clean geometric shapes; thin rules or subtle separators only where needed; a limited color palette; black-and-white imagery when photos are used; occasional accent-color highlights for emphasis, numbering, icons, or data points. The style should be functional rather than decorative.

## 2. Color palette
Primary: near black #1D1D1F; white #FFFFFF. Neutral grays: light gray #E0E0E3; medium gray #C2C2C8; dark gray/slate #575E62; muted gray #AAAEB0. Main accent: bright coral red #FF4757; darker red #E63D4B; pale red/pink #FF8792. Use the accent sparingly (number blocks, small labels, highlighted chart segments, callout markers, thin lines or icon strokes, emphasis elements). A typical slide should use only black, white, gray, and one accent color. Optional accent variants only when required: green #55FF4D/#0BF000, magenta #FE4DF9, orange #FFA73A, yellow #FFFE4E, cyan #4DFBFF — same behavior, one vivid accent used with restraint.

## 3. Typography
Use Source Sans Pro. Headlines: Light or Regular, uppercase or sentence case, left aligned, minimal, one or two lines, no decorative styling (no outlines, shadows, gradients, script/serif/condensed fonts). Strong emphasis: Bold. Body: Regular, clean and compact, left aligned, short paragraphs, medium line spacing, minimal/hyphen bullets only if necessary, bold only the key phrase. Secondary/marginal text: smaller, possibly italic. Emphasis via bold inside paragraphs; italic only for small secondary notes. No underlines, colored text blocks, excessive bolding, or mixed font families.

## 4. Image style
Photography used sparingly and editorially: black-and-white/desaturated grayscale, high contrast, documentary/institutional, cleanly cropped into strong rectangular areas, no rounded corners, no drop shadows, no decorative frames; integrated into the grid (often a large rectangle on the right). Avoid saturated color photos, glossy lifestyle stock, AI-looking portraits, gradients over photos, decorative masks.

## 5. Icons and illustrations
Simple, geometric, line-based: single-color outline icons, no/minimal fill, medium stroke, clean geometric construction, no 3D/shadows/gradients, generous space around them; accent color allowed. Functional, not decorative. Avoid cartoons, isometric scenes, multicolor icon sets, emoji-like icons, hand-drawn sketches, filled gradient pictograms.

## 6. Charts and data visualization
Minimal and quiet: white/transparent background, limited labels, neutral gray default data color, one bright accent to highlight the most important segment, thin lines, minimal gridlines, no 3D/shadows/gloss. Tables: clean grid, very light gray horizontal separators, minimal borders, accent blocks for numbering, dark text, no heavy outlines. Editorial and analytical, not dashboard-like.

## 7. Layout behavior for generated content
Easy to paste into an existing template: avoid final slide furniture (logos, footers, page numbers, fixed title placements) unless requested. Modular style: rectangular left-aligned text blocks; diagrams from simple boxes, lines, arrows, labels; clear hierarchy between primary and secondary information; generous whitespace; no crowding. Good patterns: one large content area + concise explanation; image + text; chart + short note; icon + compact paragraph; numbered items with accent number blocks; two-column with one dominant and one supporting side. Avoid complex multicolor infographics.

## 8. Shapes and graphic elements
Simple rectangular geometry: sharp-corner rectangles, thin horizontal rules, large white/light-gray highlight bars, solid black/white fields, accent-color number blocks, minimal line connectors. No rounded corners, shadows, bevels, transparency (unless extremely subtle), gradients, or ornamental patterns. On dark slides, white bands hold black text; on light slides, light-gray or accent blocks as restrained highlights.

## 9. Dark-slide style
Title/chapter slides: near-black #1D1D1F background, high-contrast white/light-gray type, large possibly oversized/cropped typographic elements, very large accent numbers in coral red, white rectangular label bands over dark, bold architectural editorial feel. No texture/gradients/vignette/decorative imagery.

## 10. Light-slide style
Content slides: white #FFFFFF background, #1D1D1F text, gray secondary elements, accent for highlights only, strong whitespace, grayscale images that don't overpower text. Calm, structured, academic.

## 11. Writing style for on-slide content
Concise, structured, presentation-friendly; clear, factual, institutional, analytical, direct, non-marketing. Avoid long prose, salesy taglines, emotional/buzzword copy, decorative slogans. Prefer hierarchy: main statement; short supporting explanation; optional source/note.

## 12. Prompt template
"Create a clean, minimal, institutional presentation graphic in the style of a modern university corporate deck. Use Source Sans Pro-style typography, strong left alignment, generous whitespace, sharp rectangular geometry, and a restrained palette of near-black #1D1D1F, white, light gray #E0E0E3, medium gray #C2C2C8, and one bright coral-red accent #FF4757. Use simple line-based icons, minimal charts, and black-and-white documentary-style imagery if photos are needed. No gradients, no shadows, no rounded corners, no decorative illustrations, no glossy effects, no 3D. Keep the content modular so it can be pasted into an existing slide template."

## 13. Negative prompt
"Do not create a colorful startup pitch deck style. Do not use gradients, glassmorphism, neumorphism, shadows, rounded cards, 3D icons, cartoon illustrations, decorative backgrounds, stock-photo color imagery, serif fonts, script fonts, excessive icons, busy infographic layouts, heavy borders, complex textures, or many accent colors. Do not include logos, footers, page numbers, full slide headlines, or branded template furniture unless explicitly requested."

## 14. Practical generation rules
One main idea per visual; max one accent color per element; prefer B/W photos; simple mostly-gray charts; thin geometric single-color icons; rectangular blocks not rounded cards; bold only for key emphasis; short labels; ample empty space; do not over-design. Output should feel like a precise modern institutional slide asset, not a poster or marketing graphic.
